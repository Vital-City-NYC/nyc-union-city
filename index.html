New York City labor

Still a union town — but a quieter one

Roughly one in five New York City workers carries a union card today, nearly double the national rate. But that share has slipped steadily for two decades, especially in the private sector — even as the union wage premium remains large in the industries where unions are strongest.

Methodology

Source: Union Membership and Coverage Database (Hirsch & Macpherson), which compiles tabulations from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' Current Population Survey. Union density figures are for New York City, 2004 and 2023–24; private-sector New York City figures are for 2014–16 and 2023–24. Wage premiums are for the New York metropolitan area, pooled 2019–21.

How to read this: Density is the share of wage and salary workers who report belonging to a union. Wage premium is the percentage difference between union and non-union median hourly wages within the named industry — it does not control for occupation, experience or education. The overall NYC premium is +3.2% (about 81 cents an hour), much smaller than the industry-specific premiums because the mix of union and non-union jobs differs across industries.

Limitations: Within-industry premium estimates can swing with composition shifts and have wider confidence bands than the headline density figure.
